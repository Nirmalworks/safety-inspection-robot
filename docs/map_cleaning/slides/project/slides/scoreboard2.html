14 map pieces of the MIT Stata Center; no model trained on any of them. Labels come from floor plans without furniture, so fixed furniture counts as clutter. Precision, F1 and accuracy: clutter as the positive class, exact cell. Session agreement and the best map need repeat maps of the same place and cannot run here.

Backup for questions on generalisation. In MIT Stata nothing passes both marks: the best that keeps 95 percent of structure removes about a third of the clutter. In standard terms: the fine-tuned erase-only U-Net falls from F1 82.6 percent in the lab to 44.3 percent here. Accuracy says little here: doing nothing already scores 84.5 percent, because only 15.5 percent of the labelled cells are clutter. ROSE2 removes the most clutter but erases a quarter of the structure, so its precision is 26.9 percent. The erase-only U-Net trained only on simulated maps has the highest F1 here, 50.2 percent, but keeps 91.3 percent of structure, below the mark. IF ASKED whether real maps of other buildings were tried in training: yes, three ways (alone, added after the simulated stage, mixed into it), with the Semantic2D and Radish datasets. None beat simulated only in MIT Stata: 13 to 34 percent of clutter removed at 95 percent structure kept, against 37 percent. EXACT-CELL RECALL OF CLUTTER, IF ASKED (the table’s clutter removed uses a 5 cm tolerance): speckle filter 27.1, ROSE2 85.4, generative simulated only 28.9, generative fine-tuned 20.2, erase-only simulated only 54.9, erase-only lab maps only 30.0, erase-only fine-tuned 34.8 percent. HOW IT WAS SCORED: figures are pooled over the 14 pieces; the two erase-only U-Nets with lab maps are the robot’s own models at their thresholds, 0.26 and 0.21; the fine-tuned generative U-Net is the mean of the four day-held-out models; the simulated-only erase-only U-Net uses its own threshold.
